Finding the few signals that matter

The question

A 110 kW motor has 26 signals. How many do you need to tell a fault?

Fewer sensors means cheaper deployment and faster models. The study compares four nature-inspired optimisers for wrapper feature selection (Whale Optimization, Harris Hawks, Equilibrium Optimizer, Multi-Verse Optimizer), each paired with LightGBM, XGBoost and CatBoost: 12 combinations, on healthy, bearing, rotor bar, stator winding and unbalance conditions.

Method

Engineer features

22 raw d-q frame signals plus 6 physics-derived ones: flux magnitudes, power per RPM, power × torque, stator voltage magnitude.

Rank with a baseline

Train full-feature models (macro F1 up to 0.999924) and keep the 15 most important features as the search space.

Search on 5%

Run each optimiser on a stratified 5% subset (105k samples), 10 agents × 20 iterations, with a sparsity penalty.

Retrain on all

Retrain each selected subset on the full 1.68M training rows and test on 420k held-out samples.

Findings

7 of 12

Rotor flux q is the key signal

It was selected most often across all classifiers, followed by RPM (6 of 12) and rotor flux d (5 of 12).

8 of 12

Physics features pay off

Engineered features such as rotor flux magnitude and power × torque appeared in 8 combinations.

HHO: always 4

Harris Hawks is the most consistent

HHO picked exactly 4 features for every classifier. EO + LightGBM got down to 3 features at macro F1 0.999688.

Remaining weakness: early bearing degradation looks electromagnetically similar to a healthy motor in the d-q frame, so most leftover errors sit between those two classes.
